About

Data science, at its core, is philosophy with a compiler. Every model is an attempt to capture what Plato called the "Forms" — the true underlying nature of things. The perfect form is always out of reach, but that's not the point. The point is what you learn on the way there.

I've spent my career doing this across industries most people treat as unrelated — healthcare, consumer goods, technology — and finding that the questions are always the same: What is really happening here? What does the data actually say? And what does that mean for the decision in front of us? At UnitedHealth Group, that meant forecasting a pandemic in 48 hours. At General Mills, it meant turning AI from a whiteboard idea into $200MM+ in business opportunity. At Meta, it means attributing invisible infrastructure investments to real revenue impact.

I think the role of data scientist is being fundamentally redefined. Tools like Claude Code have multiplied what I can do as an individual by 5–10x — and I'm not mourning the old model. I'm building in the new one.
