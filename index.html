About

I got into data science because I love getting to the bottom of things. Every dataset is a mystery — something real happened in the world, and the numbers are a clue. Building models is a bit like chasing a perfect answer you know you'll never fully reach, but that's what makes it interesting. You learn something every time, even when it doesn't work out the way you expected.

I've been lucky to chase those questions across a few very different places — healthcare, consumer goods, and technology — and what's surprised me is how much the core questions stay the same: What's actually going on here? What does this mean for the people making decisions? At UnitedHealth Group, that meant building COVID-19 forecasting tools in 48 hours. At General Mills, it meant helping turn AI from a pilot project into $200MM+ in business opportunity. At Meta, it means connecting infrastructure investments to real revenue impact.

I'm also genuinely excited about where the field is headed. AI tools like Claude Code have changed how I work — I can move faster, explore more ideas, and spend more time on the problems that actually matter. It's a great time to be a data scientist.
